Help with the iOS version

The DFE Team supports the Digital Footprint Explorer iOS app within School of Engineering ZHAW and handles privacy and support questions through the project email address below.

The iOS version is being prepared for release. These pages explain the planned customer experience and the access limits that still apply.

The garden shows changes in estimated phone use. It is a model result, rather than a direct measurement of energy use or carbon emissions.

Getting started

The automatic version needs iOS 26.4 or later and Apple's expanded Screen Time access. Open Automatic setup, read what the app will use, and allow access in Apple's permission screen. Once access is available, the app starts automatic estimates. You do not select individual apps or enter daily minutes. This setup is kept while access remains valid; if Apple later removes or replaces that access, the app can ask you to reconnect.

The app uses the application activity that Apple makes available. Local rules group recognised apps into footprint categories; other apps stay in Other apps. It does not read what you do inside an app.

Usage time is the automatic input. Data traffic, Wi-Fi share, screen brightness and GPS/Bluetooth time remain model assumptions. The app explains these assumptions alongside the estimate. Carbon results are estimates, not direct measurements.

Why has no daily result appeared yet?

Automatic estimates cover days from your setup date after each day has ended. Today's usage is still changing, so it is not imported as a completed day. Your first result can appear after a completed day has usable data.

Apple may still be preparing that day's data. iOS also decides when the app can update in the background, so updates may take time. A pending result does not mean that your usage was zero. Check Screen Time in Settings and open the app again later.

For App Store customers, Apple's expanded access requires both an iPhone located in the EU and an Apple Account set to an EU country or region. Development access used for tests in Switzerland is separate. It does not allow us to promise the same access to customers in Switzerland. If access is unavailable, the app explains the reason and leaves new results pending; it does not offer manual input.

Understanding your daily estimate

Apple's returned app activity can differ from the daily Screen Time total, even for the same day and iPhone. The estimate uses the available app durations. It does not fill a difference with invented app use or treat the result as a complete measurement of your phone's impact.

Today shows the actual completed date beside your result. Compare its imported app time with that date on the same iPhone in Apple's daily Screen Time report. Today's changing total, a weekly daily average or activity shared across devices can describe a different period. The garden compares the estimate with up to seven earlier saved days; a greener garden is a relative change, not proof of a small measured impact.

Known app identifiers use local category rules; unknown apps keep their time under Other apps. Traffic rates are category averages. The app cannot tell whether WhatsApp time was messaging or a call, which video quality you used, or how much data actually travelled. Imported activity also does not establish every hour of background music listening. Website records are excluded, while browser app time can still be included.

Automatic setup keeps the saved routine's calculation settings, including valid zero traffic assumptions. It replaces daily minutes with Apple's available app time, rather than silently changing those settings or rewriting earlier estimates. If Apple reports screen-on activity but no positive app time is available, the day stays pending; this check does not prove that all other activity was exposed. See How the estimate works in Settings for the model and its limits.

A little space for your day

There is no garden name, goal or personal profile to fill in. Once a daily estimate is available, the garden and its message reflect that result compared with up to seven earlier days. The first result is a starting point, and a pending day is never treated as zero.

A lower estimate brings a greener garden; a higher estimate changes its appearance too. One day is only part of the picture. The garden is a visual guide to estimated emissions, not a measurement of damage or recovery in the environment.

App and Home Screen appearance

In Settings, Appearance offers Light, Dark and System. Light is the default with a white background. Your choice applies across Today, History, Settings and their detail screens.

The Home Screen icon has light and dark versions, chosen by iOS. To review that choice, touch and hold the Home Screen background, then choose Edit and Customize. Select Dark for a dark icon, or Automatic to follow the system appearance. The wallpaper image and the app's own Appearance setting are separate, so a dark wallpaper alone does not choose the dark icon.

Adding the garden widget

Open the widget guide from Today or the welcome screen. For the quickest route, touch and hold the Digital Footprint app icon on your Home Screen, then tap a small or medium widget size. This turns the icon into a widget. Tap the widget to open Today.

To keep the app icon too, touch and hold an empty area of the Home Screen, then tap Edit and Add Widget. Find Digital Footprint, choose Your garden and a size, then tap Add Widget. Use this route if widget sizes do not appear in the app icon menu.

You choose the widget's position. The app cannot place it automatically. The guide can check whether one of this app's widgets is configured, but it cannot tell exactly where it is or whether its latest update is visible. This check is not saved or sent to the project team.

The widget shows the latest saved garden, estimate, date and input source. It may show an earlier date while a new result is pending. iOS controls when updates appear. Removing the widget does not remove the app's history. See Apple's widget guide for the Home Screen steps.

Optional garden updates

In Settings, under A morning moment, turn on Daily garden update if you would like a short local reminder. It is off by default and asks for Apple's notification permission only when you enable it.

DFE saves one reminder for each of the next 30 mornings, around 08:00 in your local time. iOS can show these reminders while the app is closed, including after you remove it from multitasking. Opening DFE, or background time granted by iOS, renews the schedule. If DFE cannot run for more than 30 days, the saved reminders end until it can prepare more.

A general reminder invites you to explore your latest saved garden. The wording varies with its local delivery date, without a personal profile or extra activity collection. It does not claim that yesterday's estimate is ready. Missing activity, a zero-use day or unavailable Screen Time access can still have this reminder; the app does not turn those cases into invented results.

If a fresh import provides a valid estimate for yesterday before a reminder is due, the app may replace that pending message with a summary of your saved garden. It keeps the same reminder. Older wording can also be updated in a known future request, without changing its ID or delivery time. Once the day's reminder may have appeared, a later import does not send another summary, even if you dismissed the first message. Tapping either message opens Today.

The reminder itself does not refresh Screen Time, the garden or the widgets. Removing DFE from multitasking normally stops fresh background calculations until you open it again. iOS, Focus, Scheduled Summary and notification settings can delay or hide a reminder, so delivery at exactly 08:00 or every day is not guaranteed. The schedule uses local calendar times and is checked again when DFE can run after a clock or time zone change.

These notifications stay on your iPhone, with no remote push service or activity upload. Turn Daily garden update off to cancel its pending messages. This does not disconnect Screen Time. Disconnecting Screen Time leaves the separate reminder choice unchanged, so turn the reminder off too if you do not want it. If no reminder appears, check this choice and DFE's notification settings in iPhone Settings.

Your choices and local data

To stop new Screen Time imports, choose Disconnect Screen Time in Settings. Your saved daily estimates remain available. Choose Delete all entries and confirm the warning to remove local history, saved setup, the shared widget summary, and your appearance choice.

Deleting entries also turns Daily garden update off, clears its local delivery log and cancels pending notices. Messages already shown in Notification Center stay there until you clear them.

Deleting local data does not remove Apple's Screen Time or notification permission. Use Disconnect Screen Time to remove activity access, or iPhone Settings to change notification permission. The app cannot delete Apple's own Screen Time records or recall files and backups made earlier.

The app does not send your usage history to the DFE team or a project server. History that has contained a Screen Time import cannot be exported. Read the iOS privacy policy for the reviewed local-data details.

Contact the project

For privacy or support questions, contact the DFE Team at digital-footprint-explorer.init@zhaw.ch.

If something fails, include the app version, your iOS version and a short description of the steps. Please do not send your Screen Time history, passwords, device identifier or other private files. Review any screenshot before sharing it.

If you email us, your message and email address are handled through ZHAW's email service so we can reply. Do not include private activity records. Deleting entries in the app does not remove emails you have sent; contact us if you have a question about a previous message.
